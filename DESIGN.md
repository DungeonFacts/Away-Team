Away Team is a card game where 1-4 players cooperate against a deck of challenges.

Theme:
Styled after Star Trek ‘away team’ expeditions, each player is a member of a spaceship crew (represented by a unique deck). The scenario deck is a ‘Planet’ that has some kind of challenges the crew must resolve. This could mean exploring a cave of sentient crystal creatures, retrieving medicines from a jungle of psychic plants, or preventing a war between two species on their shared homeworld.

Design Goals:
Away Team is a cooperative game about understanding and managing unfamiliar situations.
Players should feel like specialists with unique tools, not warriors with different weapons.
Most challenges should be resolved through investigation, diplomacy, preparation, or containment rather than destruction.
Players should frequently face situations where no one has the perfect answer.
The game should create emergent stories resembling a science fiction television episode.
Coordination and prediction should matter more than optimization.
A Planet is a self-contained scenario consisting of a unique deck of events. A Planet should tell a coherent story when played. Rather than win/loss games, a game resolves with branching story conclusions.

Game Cycle:
A Planet has multiple Objective tracks which are advanced by resolving Situations. Strategy is deciding how to fulfill objectives over the course of the game, tactics is deciding which Situations to address, and how to address them. Players do not interact directly with Objectives.

Basic gameplay:
Each turn, each player has a fixed set of actions: draw 1 card, play 1 card (deploy), activate 1 card. A player can take 2 actions per turn (e.g. draw two cards, play a card and activate it, or draw a card and activate one in play). Players decide which actions to take, lock in their play, and then all actions are resolved simultaneously once all players have locked in their action. The Planet deck takes its turn, resolving all the cards played by the players and then playing its own cards. Friendly fire (i.e. accidentally preventing another player’s action from resolving as anticipated) is intentionally possible! A round of Away Team should last about 15 minutes when played online, a physical card game would likely take closer to an hour due to the slower speed of resolving actions.

Tones, Natures, and Tags:
Each Objective has unique Tags that identifies what kind of narrative outcome is possible. Each card has a Tone (Hostile, Political, Mercantile, Scientific) and a Nature (Biological, Technological, Cultural, and Anomalous). This vocabulary allows for a wide range of activities and themes for each deck, such as a Hostile-focused Security Officer, a Xenobiologist with Biological focus, or a Mercantile/Technological sentient vending machine. Different decks will be stronger at resolving certain types of events, but every deck will have some tools for addressing scenarios. Not every Tone or Nature will matter with every play! Sometimes you just need to play *something* Scientific to advance a situation, but your options might have consequences if, for example, your only option is an Anomalous card that affects another situation when played.

End game conditions:
Each Planet deck has unique branching end game conditions that will be revealed as the players interact with the cards. Objectives have tracks with unique tags (e.g. ‘Criminal’ and ‘Royal’) which are Advanced by resolving Situations. A Situation can advance multiple Objectives (e.g. “Legitimate Business” advances the “Found An Outpost” objective by 1 Criminal and the “Unique Minerals” objective by 1 Mining), or none at all (e.g. a Situation called ‘Communications Down’ that is easy to resolve, but prevents Political cards from being played elsewhere while it’s in play). When all Objectives are fulfilled, the game ends.

Layout:
A player will see their hand, their currently deployed cards, the currently deployed cards of other players, and the current scenario/Planet cards. The Planet cards will be in the middle of the screen with any relevant tracking information at the top (e.g. name of the Planet, current modifiers, turn count, etc). Players cannot see each other’s hands, and for now, there isn’t any way to communicate.

Sample turn:
At the beginning of the game, the Planet T’rox has an Objective called “Discover New Life” with an “Ally” track and an “Enemy” track. On its first turn, it plays a Situation card called ‘Strange Foliage’, which has Hostile, Political, and Biological tracks, and a description reading “These plants seem to be following your moves, as if deciding whether you are friend or foe. Advance one Hostile at the end of each turn.” The Security Officer player deploys a Tactics card called “Set Phasers to Stun” and activates it. This is a Hostile/Technological card reading “Hostile tracks cannot advance this turn. Political cards have no effect this turn.” The Xenobiologist player deploys and activates “Peace Offering”, a Political/Cultural Equipment card which says “Choose one Political Situation. Advance it three times. Discard Peace Offering.” The players lock in their choices, and the locked in cards are revealed to all players. This turn, the Hostile track on the foliage does not advance, and the Security Officer’s card prevents the Xenobiologist’s card from resolving. The turn counter increments, the Planet plays a situation card called “Wandering Herbivore” with a Biological track, reading “A placid, plant eating beast wanders into the area.”

Card Types:
Planet decks have 2 card types: Objectives, and Situations. Objectives advance the core narrative of the episode, Situations are complications that can arise during the main story. Situations have at least 2 tracks to resolve, such as a Hostile track and a Mercantile track. Filling one track yields a unique resolution to that Situation. Situations are often removed from the board when they are resolved, and may specify if they have any effect (e.g. Arms Dealer might give you a Hostile/Technological card called Laser Rifle when it resolves), sometimes creating follow-up Situations, and they are the only way to Advance Objectives. Objectives remain on the board, and usually impose some kind of permanent effect (e.g. Royal Escort reduces the effect of all Hostile actions by 1, to a minimum of 1). Resolution tracks are specific to each card (e.g. the “Hostile” track on Royal Guards and Backalley Thugs are each part of the card, though some player cards might be able to increment multiple cards).

Player decks have 3 card types: Equipment (cards which will stay in play until used a finite number of times), Tactics (cards which which can be Activated any number of times), and Policies (which impose a permanent effect while in play, each player can only have one Policy active at a time). A player can have any number of Equipment and Tactics cards in play. Playing a Policy card returns any Policy in play to the player’s hand.

Scaling:
A Planet will typically have 1 Objective card in play for each player, plus 1. A Planet can have any number of Situations in play, and each one will likely have its own design basis for what they do. For example, the jungle planet T’Rox has many events that are “wandering monster” situation cards that can break gear and threaten players, while Royal Koog has events that represent different factions trying to bribe the player to help with their specific goals (none of which line up with the Royal Objectives). Unresolved events on Royal Koog are merely opportunities that might be useful later, while unresolved events on T’Rox are ongoing hazards to be addressed immediately before the crew is overwhelmed.

Glossary:

Activate: triggering the card text of a card which has previously been deployed.

Card: the fundamental unit of gameplay. Each player has a deck of cards, and the Planet puts cards into play representing Situations, Objectives, and any resultant creations thereof.

Deploy: playing a card from a player’s hand.

Emerge: playing a card from the Planet deck.

Fulfill: to complete a track on an Objective or Situation card.

Nature: the intrinsic descriptor of a card. The four categories are Biological, Technological, Cultural, and Anomalous. Every player card has a Nature.

Objective: key cards to completing Planet storylines. An Objective typically has a track with a unique Tag (such as ‘Royal’ and ‘Criminal’) which are Advanced by resolving Situations. A game ends when all Objective tracks are fulfilled.

Planet: the scenario which all players face during a game. A Planet is not a ‘boss’ or enemy, but a collection of challenges that can be resolved in multiple ways to generate a story and resolve one of multiple possible endings.

Resolve: when a Situation or Objective card has one of its tracks Fulfilled, it Resolves. This can mean removing it from the board, spawning a subsequent Situation or Objective, or staying on the board and creating some kind of ongoing effect.

Tag: Planet-unique qualities for Objective tracks, distinct from Tone and Nature. As an example, “Establish an Outpost” is a Royal Koog Objective with a Royal and Criminal track. These tracks can only be Advanced by resolving Situations, not by the player directly.

Tone: the categorical descriptor of a card. The four Tones are Hostile, Political, Mercantile, and Scientific. Every player card has a Tone.

Track: a counter on a Situation or Objective card. These tracks are Advanced (increased in value) or Reduced (decreased in value) by player actions. A track is Fulfilled when it reaches its maximum value, and the card resolves accordingly.
