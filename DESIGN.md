Away Team is a card game where 1-4 players cooperate against a deck of challenges.

Theme:
Styled after Star Trek ‘away team’ expeditions, each player is a member of a spaceship crew (represented by a unique deck). The scenario deck is a ‘Planet’ that has some kind of challenges the crew must resolve. This could mean exploring a cave of sentient crystal creatures, retrieving medicines from a jungle of psychic plants, or preventing a war between two species on their shared homeworld.

Design Goals:
Away Team is a cooperative game about understanding and managing unfamiliar situations.
Players should feel like specialists with unique tools, not warriors with different weapons.
Most challenges should be resolved through investigation, diplomacy, preparation, or containment rather than destruction.
Players should frequently face situations where no one has the perfect answer.
The game should create emergent stories resembling a science fiction television episode.
Coordination and prediction should matter more than optimization.
A Planet is a self-contained scenario consisting of a unique deck of events, a victory condition, and one or more failure conditions. A Planet should tell a coherent story when played.

Basic gameplay:
Each turn, each player has a fixed set of actions: draw 1 card, play 1 card (deploy), activate 1 card. A player can take 2 actions per turn (e.g. draw two cards, play a card and activate it, or draw a card and activate one in play). Players decide which actions to take, lock in their play, and then all actions are resolved simultaneously once all players have locked in their action. The Planet deck takes its turn, resolving all the cards played by the players and then playing its own cards. The game ends when the scenario is either won or failed. A round of Away Team should last about 15 minutes when played online, a physical card game would likely take closer to an hour due to the slower speed of resolving actions.

Tag based play:
Each scenario and card has a Tag that identifies what kind of resource or challenge it is. Each card has a Tone (Hostile, Diplomatic, Mercantile, Scientific) and a Nature (Biological, Technological, Cultural, and Anomalous). This vocabulary allows for a wide range of activities and themes for each deck, such as a Hostile-focused Security Officer, a Xenobiologist with Biological focus, or a Mercantile/Technological sentient vending machine. Different decks will be stronger at resolving certain types of events, but every deck will have some tools for addressing scenarios. Not every tag will matter with every play! Sometimes you just need to play *something* Scientific to advance an objective, but your options might have consequences if, for example, your only option is an Anomalous card that affects another objective when played.

Win and Loss conditions:
Each Planet deck has unique win and loss conditions that will be revealed as the players interact with the cards. Broadly speaking, each Objective card from the planet deck has a status level that is incremented by player actions, e.g. ‘Royal Bodyguard’ has a Hostile track and a Diplomatic diplomatic track, and resolves when it reaches 3 points in either direction, becoming “Royal Escort” if resolved with Diplomacy, or “Criminal Probation” if resolved with Hostile. Roughly speaking, a planet will have 1 core objective, which is unlocked by resolving a sufficient number of randomly drawn objectives, at which point the players can attempt to resolve the core objective. In our example above, “A Royal Audience” is the core objective, requires 3 of a possible 5 resolutions with Royal in the name, and becomes impossible if the players accrue 3 resolutions with Criminal in the name.

Layout:
A player will see their hand, their currently deployed cards, the currently deployed cards of other players, and the current scenario/Planet cards. The Planet cards will be in the middle of the screen with any relevant tracking information at the top (e.g. name of the Planet, current modifiers, turn count, etc). Players cannot see each other’s hands, and for now, there isn’t any way to communicate.

Sample turn:
At the beginning of the game, the Planet T’rox plays an objective card called ‘Strange Foliage’, which has Hostile, Diplomatic, and Biological tags, and a description reading “These plants seem to be following your moves, as if deciding whether you are friend or foe. Advance one Hostile at the end of each turn.” The Security Officer player deploys a Tactics card called “Set Phasers to Stun” and activates it. This is a Hostile/Technological card reading “Hostile events cannot advance this turn. Diplomatic cards do not resolve this turn.” The Xenobiologist player deploys and activates “Peace Offering”, a Diplomatic/Cultural Equipment card which says “Choose one Diplomacy Objective. Advance it three times. Discard Peace Offering.” The players lock in their choices, and the locked in cards are revealed to all players. This turn, the Hostile track on the foliage does not advance, and the Security Officer’s card prevents the Xenobiologist’s card from resolving. The turn counter increments, the Planet plays an event card called “Wandering Herbivore” with a Biological tag, reading “A placid, plant eating beast wanders into the area.”

Card Types:
Planet decks have 2 card types: Objectives, and Events. Objectives advance the core narrative of the episode, Events are complications that can arise during the main story. Events are removed from the board when they are resolved, and may specify if they have any effect (e.g. Arms Dealer might give you a Hostile/Technological card called Laser Rifle when it resolves). Objectives remain on the board, and usually impose some kind of permanent effect (e.g. Royal Escort reduces the effect of all Hostile actions by 1, to a minimum of 1). Resolution tracks are specific to each card (e.g. the “Hostile” track on Royal Guards and Backalley Thugs are each part of the card, though some player cards might be able to increment multiple cards).

Player decks have 3 card types: Equipment (cards which will stay in play until used a finite number of times), Tactics (cards which which can be Activated any number of times), and Policies (which impose a permanent effect while in play, each player can only have one Policy active at a time). A player can have any number of Equipment and Tactics cards in play. Playing a Policy card returns any Policy in play to the player’s hand.

Scaling:
A Planet will typically have 1 Objective card in play for each player, plus 1. A Planet can have any number of Events in play, and each one will likely have its own design basis for what they do. For example, the jungle planet T’Rox has many events that are “wandering monster” cards that can break gear and threaten players, while Royal Koog has events that represent different factions trying to bribe the player to help with their specific goals (none of which line up with the Royal Objectives). Unresolved events on Royal Koog are merely opportunities that might be useful later, while unresolved events on T’Rox are ongoing hazards to be addressed immediately before the crew is overwhelmed.

Prototype goals:
2 player decks (Security Officer and Xenobiologist)
1 Planet (Royal Koog)
10 Planet cards (objectives and events)
20 cards per player deck
No deck-building (to be added later, each Officer is a core deck, and eventually you can splash a small number of cards from neutral cards or another Officer)
Win by getting 3 Royal cards, lose by accumulating 3 Criminal cards.
